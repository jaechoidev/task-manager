# Findings: running several verifies at once, and `factory capacity` (2026-11-24)

Two questions came before any work on running several projects on one machine. Can several verify runs share the machine, each on its own simulator? And can the machine say how many it can take?

## Result

Yes to both. Three verify runs at once took 12% longer each than one alone in this measurement, with every flow passing. A second measurement the same day had 41 to 46%. `factory capacity` reads the machine and proposes how much to run at once.

The measurement: the trial app's `verify.sh` (a cold build, install, and all 8 Maestro flows), each run in its own checkout on its own simulator, on a 12-core M2 Max with 96 GB of memory.

| At once | Wall time | Each run | Flows | Verifies per hour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 196 s | 196 s | 8 of 8 | 18 |
| 2 | 203 s | 198 s, 202 s | 8 of 8 on both | 35 |
| 3 | 219 s | 206 s, 212 s, 219 s | 8 of 8 on all three | 49 |

- **The 12% did not repeat.** Measured again the same afternoon, with the same app and commit, three at once took 277 to 287 s each, 41 to 46% longer than one alone (197 s). See [pools](2026-11-24-pools.md). Read the table above as the best case: three slots gave between 2.1 and 2.7 times the throughput of one.
- **Separate Maestro runs do not collide.** Maestro's documentation only covers one process spreading flows over several devices. Three separate processes, each given its own device, ran with no port or launch error.
- **A verify of this app is mostly waiting.** Of 196 seconds, about 18 are the build and install. The rest is Maestro driving the screen. That is why three at once cost so little.

## What a simulator costs

| | Measured |
| --- | --- |
| Memory per booted simulator | About 8 GB, with what it caches. Free memory went from 71% to 46% with three booted |
| Disk per simulator, after one use | 2.7 GB |
| First use of a new simulator | 239 to 243 s for the same verify, against 196 s. The load average stayed between 120 and 300 for minutes |
| CPU with three simulators booted and idle | 70% idle |

Two things follow for the engine:

- **Simulators must be made and used once ahead of time.** A simulator's first boot runs indexing and migration inside it. Doing that during a run would slow every other issue on the machine.
- **The load average is the wrong signal.** With three idle simulators it read 7 while 70% of the CPU was idle, and 250 during a first boot. `factory capacity` reports it and does not use it. The live check uses the CPU's idle share, sampled over a second.

## What `factory capacity` proposes

| Limit | Rule | On the measured machine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| By cores | 4 per simulator step, from all cores | 3 |
| By memory | 8 GB per step, with 8 GB kept back | 11 |
| Ceiling | 3, the most that was measured | 3 |
| Agent calls per vendor | 2. A subscription's usage limit bounds this, not the machine | 2 |

For this minute, it takes what is busy off the allowance: a step's worth for every 4 busy cores, ignoring the first one. Below 15% free memory it allows nothing.

It also checks what a run depends on: Xcode, Command Line Tools matching macOS, Maestro, Java, git, `gh` and its login, `codex` and `claude`. On the Mac Studio that is the first thing to run.

## Problems hit

1. **The first version counted 1 iPhone simulator.** Xcode has a second iOS 27.2 runtime with a device-specific name, and its key sorted as the newest. Simulators are now counted across every runtime of the newest iOS version.
2. **The first live check allowed nothing on an idle machine.** It divided idle cores by 4 and rounded down, so 95% idle on 12 cores gave 2 where the proposal said 3. It now subtracts what is busy from the allowance.
3. **The first constants were guesses,** 3 cores and 6 GB per step. They were replaced by what was measured.

## Open items

- **The proposal is not used yet.** The engine still holds one `simulator` lock. Pools are the next step. (Built later the same day: [pools](2026-11-24-pools.md).)
- **One app, one machine.** The trial app builds in 18 seconds. A real project's `xcodebuild` can hold every core for minutes, and then cores, not waiting, decide how many fit. The ceiling of 3 is the most that was tried, not a limit that was found.
- **No measuring mode.** The measurement was a script run by hand. `factory capacity --measure` would run a project's own verify 1, 2 and 3 at once and size the pools from that.
- **Three simulators named `factory-sim-1` to `factory-sim-3` were left on this machine,** shut down, about 8 GB of disk in all. They are already past their slow first use, and pools would need them.
- **Not checked: whether `codex` and `claude` are logged in.** Only that they are installed.
